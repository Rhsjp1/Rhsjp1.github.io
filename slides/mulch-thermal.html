Dark mulch hits 150°F in July. Your plant roots are boiling.
Dark-colored mulch in full sun absorbs 90% of solar radiation. Surface temperatures above 130°F damage root hairs and kill soil biology in the top 2 inches.
📊
2 / 7
Not all mulch is created equal. Temperature varies 15-30°F by type.
Shredded hardwood: 145°F. Straw mulch: 118°F. Pine straw over wood chips: 112°F. Your choice of mulch is a thermal decision, not just aesthetic.
📈
3 / 7
Mulch depth is a thermostat. 2 inches vs. 4 inches changes everything.
Too thin: soil surface temp spikes above 100°F. Too thick: anaerobic decomposition generates heat and kills roots. The 3-inch sweet spot insulates without cooking.
🎯
4 / 7
The planner maps your property's solar exposure by zone.
South-facing beds receive 4x the solar radiation of north-facing ones. The planner scores each zone and recommends mulch type, depth, and color matched to thermal load.
📊
5 / 7
Heat islands aren't just a city problem. Your driveway creates one.
Asphalt and concrete absorb and re-radiate heat, creating microclimates 15-20°F hotter than open turf. Adjacent planting beds need thermal-resistant mulch strategies.
📈
6 / 7
Mulch color is a lever most property managers never touch.
Switching from black to natural brown mulch reduces surface temp by 20-25°F. The planner recommends color-by-zone so every bed stays in the biological safety zone.
🎯
